## 1. The Problem

Every profession depends on records. Lawyers have case files. Doctors have medical records. Businesses have accounting systems. Artists, despite creating assets that may appreciate over decades, still rely primarily on certificates of authenticity to document ownership and authorship.

Whether issued as paper certificates or PDFs, these records are not self-verifying. A PDF can be altered as easily as a paper certificate can be forged, and neither provides a tamper-proof record that can be trusted on its own. Authenticity still depends on external verification. Several US artists' foundations stopped issuing certificates altogether after legal disputes involving fraudulent works that carried them.

The global art market reached an estimated $59.6B in 2025, while the Art Loss Register estimates forged artwork accounts for nearly 10% of it. Against that backdrop, an entire profession of independent authenticators exists simply to determine whether a work is genuine.

That profession scales with an artwork's value. It is common at the high end of the market and largely inaccessible to emerging artists and lower-value transactions, the exact segment TRAC is built for. It also represents money leaving the transaction entirely, paid to a third-party intermediary rather than remaining within the relationship between artist and collector where it originated.

Its existence is evidence of a broader infrastructure gap. If ownership and authenticity could already be established reliably through existing records, an independent authentication industry would not need to exist at any price point.

Today, that gap is filled informally through paper certificates, verbal assurance, and relationship-based verification. None of these methods withstand serious scrutiny, and none scale.

Existing blockchain-based solutions do not close this gap, they relocate it. They require artists to learn wallets, gas fees, and crypto workflows simply to prove ownership of their own work, replacing one burden with another. The administrative weight never actually leaves the artist.

**What this means for TRACStudio:** solving this problem is a legitimate cost of doing business, no different from any professional maintaining records or paying for tools. The issue with existing blockchain-based fixes isn't that they ask artists to do anything at all, it's that the specific overhead they require is disproportionate to the actual problem being solved and unrelated to the skill of being an artist. A lawyer does not need to understand how a database works to use case-management software. An artist should not need to understand blockchain to issue a tamper-proof certificate. TRACStudio closes that gap by making blockchain invisible while keeping ownership infrastructure under the artist's control from the outset. In practice, this gives artists leverage over authentication and collectors confidence that a purchase is genuine without requiring technical knowledge.

## 2. The Broader Opportunity

TRACStudio begins with a single record of an artwork. Everything else grows from that foundation.

The information an artist already enters while documenting a piece can also power a portfolio, archive, authenticity record, provenance history, and eventually verified secondary-market royalties. Rather than asking artists to adopt separate tools for each task, TRACStudio increases the value of the same record over time.

Each layer has independent value. Together, they create infrastructure that does not currently exist for most artists.

1. **Archive.** Artists require a permanent record of their work over time. TRACStudio provides this as a byproduct of normal use.
2. **Portfolio.** The same underlying data becomes a free, professional portfolio requiring no design skill.
3. **Authentication (COA).** The same record extends into a blockchain-backed certificate controlled by the artist, with blockchain fully abstracted from the user.
4. **Provenance.** Over time, the artwork's history grows beyond the point of sale, strengthening authenticity and laying the foundation for verified secondary-market royalties.
5. **Process history.** The long-term direction of the product is capturing artistic process, from ideation through completion, as a natural byproduct of creating work. A finished piece can be copied. Its documented development cannot.
6. **Network effects.** As more artists build their practice on the same foundation, TRACStudio naturally develops a growing network of verified artworks and provenance records, creating value that extends beyond any single feature.
7. **Audience monetization.** Advertising, partnerships, and artist-relevant services provide an additional revenue path that depends only on an engaged user base rather than authentication adoption.

None of these layers depend on the others succeeding. If institutional adoption of COAs is slow, the portfolio and archive layers retain independent value. If blockchain-based monetization underperforms, advertising against an engaged user base remains viable. If the full-process record takes years to build out, the core portfolio-to-COA flow already functions today. This is resilience, not a feature list.

## 3. The Product

TRACStudio is a single system built around a single record of an artwork, not a collection of separate features.

- **Portfolio and archive tool** (free): artists upload artworks, build a public portfolio, and maintain a permanent record of their work.
- **COA issuance:** a blockchain-backed certificate of authenticity, initiated and controlled by the artist, with all blockchain complexity hidden from the user.

The portfolio is the foundation of the system. The information required to issue a COA, including artist identity, artwork details, and provenance, is the same information an artist already enters while documenting their work and building a portfolio. There is no separate onboarding process for authentication.

Artists start with TRACStudio because they need a portfolio. Authentication is simply the next step in the same workflow, not a separate product they have to learn.

**Key distinction:** TRACStudio's COAs are not NFTs. They are never listed for sale, never transferred between wallets, and never represent ownership of a digital asset. Each COA is owned by the issuing artist and held in custodial storage by TRACStudio. The certificate itself never changes hands. Instead, verified ownership changes of the physical artwork are recorded through an append-only on-chain provenance log, preserving the artist as the source of truth while creating a permanent history of ownership.

**Value to collectors:** confidence that a purchase is genuine, backed by a tamper-proof record instead of a paper certificate that can be lost or forged. No blockchain knowledge is required. Collectors simply sign in to their TRACStudio Collector's dashboard to access their records. Every verified acquisition becomes part of the artwork's permanent provenance history, creating a continuous chain of artist-authorized ownership.

## 4. How It Works

1. The artist uploads artwork and metadata to TRACStudio while building their portfolio. This creates the artwork's permanent record.

- At the point of sale, TRACStudio mints a single COA on the artist's behalf. The certificate contains the artwork's metadata and remains permanently held by TRACStudio as the canonical authenticity record.
- The collector receives access to the artwork through a TRACStudio Collector's dashboard via email. No wallet or blockchain knowledge is required.
- TRACStudio records the verified ownership change by appending a new entry to the artwork's on-chain provenance log. Each entry contains a platform-generated collector identifier, a timestamp, and the verified transfer sequence. The COA itself never moves.
- On resale, through a gallery or auction house, the institution requests access to the artwork's TRACStudio record to verify both authenticity and the complete provenance history before completing the transaction. Following verification, TRACStudio appends the new ownership record to the provenance log.
- Every verified transfer strengthens the artwork's provenance record, creating a continuous, artist-authorized ownership history that supports authentication today and verified secondary-market royalties in the future.
- Corrections use a burn-and-replace process for the COA, while preserving a reference to the original record so provenance remains continuous.
- Artist unavailability (inactivity or death) is handled through estate or delegate authority.

**"Invisible blockchain" is the core value proposition.** TRACStudio absorbs the complexity of wallets, keys, and gas fees so that artists and collectors never interact with crypto directly. This abstraction is the paid service.

## 5. Why This Approach Works

- **Meets the art market where it already operates.** Artists, collectors, galleries, and auction houses continue working within familiar sales processes. TRACStudio strengthens those workflows rather than requiring the market to adopt crypto first.
- **Leverage-based rather than surveillance-based.** TRACStudio does not attempt to police every resale. It focuses on the transactions that matter most, gallery and auction house sales where authenticity and provenance directly influence value.
- **Digitizes an existing trust relationship.** Artists already serve as the primary source of authenticity for their own work. TRACStudio preserves that relationship by giving artists ownership of their certificates while providing the infrastructure to verify them over time.
- **Separates authenticity from ownership.** The COA belongs to the artist and remains permanently in custodial storage managed by TRACStudio. Collectors never receive or own the certificate itself. Instead, every verified acquisition becomes a new entry in the artwork's append-only provenance record, creating a continuous history of artist-authorized ownership without transferring the certificate.

## 6. Go-to-Market Strategy

TRACStudio's go-to-market strategy is built around a simple insight: artists adopt tools that immediately improve how they present and manage their work, not abstract infrastructure they don't yet understand. Rather than leading with authentication, TRACStudio begins with a free, professional portfolio that any artist can use immediately.

This free portfolio becomes the platform's primary acquisition channel, as artists adopt TRACStudio to solve an immediate need while authentication becomes a natural extension of the same workflow rather than a separate product to learn. As artists upload work, they are already creating structured, verifiable artwork records in the background, even if they are not yet thinking about provenance, ownership, or certificates of authenticity.

The first validation of this workflow is already underway through a closed test with a small group of artists, allowing the portfolio experience to be refined before public release. In parallel, the COA workflow will be tested separately with an artist holding an actual residency and exhibition, providing the first end-to-end validation of certificate issuance, collector onboarding, custodial management, and on-chain provenance logging.

From there, TRACStudio expands through a bottom-up network effect. Artists share their portfolios publicly, collectors and curators interact with verified works, and galleries and auction houses begin referencing TRACStudio records during sales and resale. Every verified transaction strengthens the provenance network, increasing the value of verification for everyone already participating.

Over time, TRACStudio evolves from a portfolio platform into the default provenance and authenticity layer for contemporary art, without requiring artists, collectors, or institutions to fundamentally change how they already work.

## 7. Monetization

TRACStudio's monetization model is transaction-based rather than subscription-based, aligning costs with the way artists actually earn income. Instead of charging recurring fees regardless of whether an artist makes a sale, TRACStudio generates revenue only when value is created.

1. **COA issuance fee**, charged when a Certificate of Authenticity is issued.
2. **Verified secondary-sale fee**, collected when a gallery or auction house completes a resale through TRACStudio's verified provenance workflow.
3. **Correction fee**, applied when an artist requests a legitimate metadata correction through the burn-and-reissue process.
4. **Audience monetization**, including artist-relevant advertising, partnerships, and services that depend on an engaged free-tier user base rather than authentication adoption.

This structure ensures artists can use TRACStudio's core portfolio and archive features at no cost while paying only when authentication or verified transactions provide measurable value. As provenance records mature and institutional verification becomes more common, transaction-based revenue naturally scales alongside activity rather than user count.

Payment processing is intentionally outside the scope of the initial product. TRACStudio will integrate with established payment providers as transaction volume grows, allowing the platform to remain focused on authentication and provenance rather than financial infrastructure.

## 8. Technical Notes (Diligence Readiness)

### Platform Architecture

- Backend: Supabase.
- Frontend: TRACStudio (tracstudio.app).
- Blockchain: Polygon, selected for low transaction costs and support for richer on-chain metadata than would be practical on Ethereum mainnet.

### Proposed Smart Contract Architecture

- One Certificate of Authenticity (COA) is issued for each artwork.
- Only the TRACStudio platform wallet has authority to mint new COAs.
- Every COA is issued to the artist's custodial account managed by TRACStudio.
- COAs are permanently non-transferable and cannot be bought, sold, or transferred between wallets.
- Ownership of the physical artwork is represented separately through an append-only provenance log linked to each COA.
- Only the TRACStudio platform wallet may append new provenance records after verifying a legitimate sale or resale.
- Each provenance entry records a platform-generated collector identifier, timestamp, and ownership sequence.
- The artist remains the owner of the certificate throughout its lifetime, while TRACStudio serves as custodian of the blockchain record and administrator of provenance updates.

### Security

Security and architecture hardening of the application backend has already been completed, including row-level security across core tables, ownership validation on destructive actions, storage bucket lockdown, indexing and foreign-key improvements, and general dataflow and QA cleanup.

### Open Technical and Legal Questions

- **Event logs vs. on-chain storage.** Polygon's low transaction costs make either approach feasible. Event-based provenance provides significantly lower gas costs and scales better, while on-chain storage enables direct contract queries without indexing infrastructure. The current preference is event-based provenance indexed by TRACStudio's backend, with only essential contract state stored on-chain.
- **Custody model.** TRACStudio acts as custodian of artist-owned certificates while retaining exclusive authority over minting and provenance updates. The custody model, multisignature controls, key management, disaster recovery, and operational governance require formal security and legal review.
- **Correction workflow.** Metadata corrections will use a burn-and-reissue process that preserves a cryptographic reference to the previous certificate, ensuring provenance remains continuous while maintaining an immutable audit trail.
- **Estate authority.** Procedures are required for transferring certificate custody and provenance authority to an artist's estate or delegated representative in cases of death or incapacity.
- **Smart contract review.** The proposed architecture reflects the intended product design and has not yet undergone formal review by a dedicated smart contract security specialist. Contract design, gas optimization, and legal implications will be validated before production deployment.

The goal is not to simulate adoption, but to observe how the platform performs within an active artistic community before expanding to a wider audience.
